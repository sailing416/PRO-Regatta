NOR Publication Deadline — Plan Ahead
The NOR must be published far enough in advance to give competitors a fair and reasonable opportunity to enter. RRS Rule 89.2(a) requires the organizing authority to publish a NOR before the event. Sail Canada and World Sailing recommend a minimum of 6–8 weeks for major events and at least 2–4 weeks for club regattas. A late NOR creates real problems: competitors cannot arrange travel, boats, or crew; eligibility requirements may be unenforceable; entry fees may not be recoverable. If the NOR changes after publication, RRS Rule 89.2(b) controls what changes are permitted and how they must be communicated. Use this tool before you publish — not after.

NOR Requirements Guide
Notice of Race · RRS Rule 89.2 · Sail Canada Prescriptions
📄
The Notice of Race is the first official document of a regatta. Under RRS Rule 89.2(a), the organizing authority is required to publish a NOR. Use the AI Assistant on the right to ask any question about NOR requirements.
⚠️
The NOR is a binding document. If a NOR provision conflicts with the Sailing Instructions, the SIs take precedence — but only where they are permitted to change the NOR. Under Sail Canada Prescription 88.2, Sail Canada's Prescriptions to Part 5 and Rule 65.1 cannot be changed or deleted by the NOR or SIs under any circumstances.
📖
The NOR content requirements come from three sources: RRS Rule 89.2 (the organizing authority shall publish a NOR), RRS Appendix J1 (the detailed content lists), and the Sail Canada Prescriptions. The items below quote the actual rule text from Appendix J. In this appendix, the term "event" includes a race or series of races. A rule in the NOR need not be repeated in the SIs.

J1.1 — The NOR shall include the following App J1.1

These six items are mandatory in every NOR without exception. The word "shall" is used — there is no discretion.

J1.1(1) — Title, Place, Dates and Organizing Authority ▾

Rule text: "The title, place and dates of the event and name of the organizing authority."

  • ★The full title of the event
  • ★The place (location / sailing area)
  • ★The dates of the event
  • ★The name of the organizing authority
J1.1(2) — Governed by the Racing Rules of Sailing ▾

Rule text: "That the event will be governed by the rules as defined in The Racing Rules of Sailing."

This must appear as a statement in the NOR. It is not sufficient to simply list rules — the NOR must state that the event is governed by the RRS.

J1.1(3) — Other Governing Documents ▾

Rule text: "A list of any other documents that will govern the event (for example, The Equipment Rules of Sailing, to the extent that they apply), stating where or how each document or an electronic copy of it may be obtained."

  • ★List every additional governing document — class rules, Equipment Rules of Sailing, handicap system rules, measurement rules, etc.
  • ★State where or how each document (or an electronic copy) may be obtained
J1.1(4) — Classes, Handicap System, Conditions of Entry ▾

Rule text: "The classes to race, any handicap or rating system that will be used, and the classes to which it will apply; conditions of entry and any restrictions on entries."

  • ★The class or classes that will race
  • ★Any handicap or rating system that will be used and which classes it applies to
  • ★Conditions of entry — what a competitor must satisfy to enter
  • ★Any restrictions on entries
  • ★Sail Canada Prescription Rule 46: The person in charge, if resident in Canada, must be a member of Sail Canada through either a club affiliated to Sail Canada or their Provincial Sailing Association Sail Canada Rule 46
J1.1(5) — Registration, Entry Procedures, Fees and Closing Dates ▾

Rule text: "The procedures and times for registration or entry, including fees and any closing dates."

  • ★Registration or entry procedures — how to enter and where
  • ★Times for registration
  • ★Entry fees (if any)
  • ★Closing date for entries
J1.1(6) — Warning Signal Times ▾

Rule text: "The times of warning signals for the practice race, if one is scheduled, and the first race, and succeeding races if known."

  • ★Time of the warning signal for the practice race (if scheduled)
  • ★Time of the warning signal for the first race
  • ★Times for succeeding races if known

J1.2 — The NOR shall include the following if they will apply App J1.2

These items are mandatory if they apply to your event. Review each one and include all that are relevant. Omitting an applicable item is a compliance error.

J1.2(1) — Equipment Inspection or Measurement ▾

Rule text: "Times or procedures for equipment inspection or event measurement, or requirements for measurement or rating certificates."

J1.2(2) — Changes to Racing Rules Authorized by World Sailing ▾

Rule text: "Changes to the racing rules authorized by World Sailing under rule 86.2, referring specifically to each rule and stating the change (also include the statement from World Sailing authorizing the change)." RRS 86.2

J1.2(3) — Changes to Class Rules ▾

Rule text: "Changes to class rules, as permitted under rule 87, referring specifically to each rule and stating the change." RRS 87

J1.2(4) — Categorization or Classification Requirements ▾

Rule text: "Categorization or classification requirements that some or all competitors must satisfy:"

  • ★(a) For sailor categorization — see rule 79 and the World Sailing Sailor Categorization Code RRS 79
  • ★(b) For functional classification for Para World Sailing events — see World Sailing Para Classification Rules
J1.2(5) — Advertising ▾

Rule text: "That boats will be required to display advertising chosen and supplied by the organizing authority (see rule 6 and the World Sailing Advertising Code) and other information related to advertising." RRS 6, App G

Sail Canada Prescription Appendix G: Boats not in a World Sailing Class or other recognized class whose overall length exceeds 6 metres and which race under a measurement rule or handicapping system shall carry numbers allotted by a sailing national authority on mainsails, spinnakers and each overlapping headsail having a luff perpendicular measurement exceeding 130% of the base of the boat's foretriangle. Sail Canada App G

J1.2(6) — Rule 90.3(e) and Time Limit Changes ▾

Rule text: "That rule 90.3(e) will apply, and any change in the '24 hours' time limit in that rule." RRS 90.3(e)

J1.2(7) — National Prescriptions Requiring Advance Preparation ▾

Rule text: "When entries from other countries are expected, any national prescriptions that may require advance preparation (see rule 88)." RRS 88

J1.2(8) — Multi-Jurisdiction Prescriptions ▾

Rule text: "Prescriptions that will apply if boats will pass through the waters of more than one national authority while racing, and when they will apply (see rule 88.1)." RRS 88.1

J1.2(9) — Alternative Communication for Rule 20 Hails ▾

Rule text: "Alternative communication required in place of hails under rule 20 (see rule 20.4(b))." RRS 20.4(b)

J1.2(10) — Change in Weight Limit for Clothing and Equipment ▾

Rule text: "Any change in the weight limit for a competitor's clothing and equipment permitted by rule 50.1(b)." RRS 50.1(b)

J1.3 — The NOR shall include any of the following that will apply and that would help competitors decide whether to attend App J1.3

These items must be included if they apply. They are also the items that competitors need before the SIs are available — include them if they affect a competitor's decision to enter or prepare.

J1.3(1) — Changes to Racing Rules ▾

Rule text: "Changes to the racing rules permitted by rule 86.1, referring specifically to each rule and stating the change." RRS 86.1

J1.3(2) — Changes to National Prescriptions ▾

Rule text: "Changes to the national prescriptions (see rule 88.2)." RRS 88.2

Sail Canada Prescription 88.2: Sail Canada prescribes that its prescriptions to Part 5 of the rules and rule 65.1 may not be changed or deleted. The NOR or SIs may change or delete any other Sail Canada prescription only if a no-appeal protest committee or an international jury has been appointed for the event. Sail Canada 88.2

J1.3(3) — Time and Place for Sailing Instructions ▾

Rule text: "The time and place at which the sailing instructions will be available."

J1.3(4) — General Course Description ▾

Rule text: "A general description of the course, or type of courses, to be sailed."

J1.3(5) — Scoring System ▾

Rule text: "The scoring system, if different from the system in Appendix A, included by reference to class rules or other rules governing the event, or stated in full. State the number of races scheduled and the minimum number that must be scored to constitute a series (see rule A1). If appropriate, for a series where the number of starters may vary substantially, state that rule A5.3 applies." RRS App A, A1, A5.3

  • ★If using Appendix A Low Point scoring — state this
  • ★If using a different scoring system — state it in full or reference the applicable rule
  • ★Number of races scheduled
  • ★Minimum number of races that must be scored to constitute a series
J1.3(6) — Penalty for Breaking a Rule of Part 2 ▾

Rule text: "The penalty for breaking a rule of Part 2, other than the Two-Turns Penalty." RRS 44

If the event will use a One-Turn Penalty, a scoring penalty, or Appendix P, this must be stated in the NOR.

J1.3(7) — Time After Which No Warning Signal on Last Day ▾

Rule text: "The time after which no warning signal will be made on the last scheduled day of racing."

J1.3(8) — Denial of Right of Appeal ▾

Rule text: "Denial of the right of appeal, subject to rule 70.3." RRS 70.3, 70.5

If the right of appeal will be denied, this must be clearly stated in the NOR and a no-appeal protest committee or international jury must be appointed. Sail Canada Prescription 70.3(b) so prescribes.

Sail Canada Prescription 91(b): Before making a request to World Sailing for an international jury formed under rule N1.7 comprised of only three members, written approval must be obtained from Sail Canada — submit to [email protected]. Sail Canada 91(b)

J1.3(9) — Chartered or Loaned Boats and Rule G3 ▾

Rule text: "For chartered or loaned boats, whether rule G3 applies." RRS G3

J1.3(10) — Prizes ▾

Rule text: "Prizes."

State the prizes offered. If no prizes are offered, state that as well so competitors have clear information before deciding to attend.

Sail Canada Prescriptions — Mandatory for Canadian Events Sail Canada Prescriptions

The following Sail Canada Prescriptions apply to all Canadian events and must be reflected in the NOR. Under Sail Canada Prescription 88.2, the prescriptions to Part 5 and Rule 65.1 cannot be changed or deleted under any circumstances.

Rule 46 — Person in Charge Membership Requirement ▾

Prescription text: "Sail Canada prescribes that the designated person in charge, if resident in Canada, shall be a member of Sail Canada through either a club affiliated to Sail Canada or such person's Provincial Sailing Association." Sail Canada Rule 46

Part 5 — No Fee for Protest Committee Procedures ▾

Prescription text: "Sail Canada prescribes that no fee shall be charged for any procedure of the protest committee under Part 5 or Appendix T." Sail Canada Part 5

This prescription cannot be changed or deleted by the NOR or SIs under any circumstances.

Rule 65.1 — Legal Liability and Costs ▾

Prescription text: "Sail Canada prescribes that a boat that has been found by a protest committee to have broken a rule and caused damage shall be considered at fault for the purposes of rule 65.1." Sail Canada 65.1

This prescription cannot be changed or deleted by the NOR or SIs under any circumstances.

Rule 88.2 — What Can and Cannot Be Changed ▾

Prescription text: "Sail Canada prescribes that its prescriptions to Part 5 of the rules and rule 65.1 may not be changed or deleted. The notice of race or sailing instructions may change or delete any other Sail Canada prescription only if a no-appeal protest committee or an international jury has been appointed for the event." Sail Canada 88.2

SI Requirements Guide
Sailing Instructions · RRS Rules 86, 89 · Sail Canada Prescriptions
📘
Sailing Instructions are the detailed operating rules for a specific regatta. They must be consistent with the NOR. Under RRS Rule 86.1, the SIs may only change rules that are listed as changeable.
⚠️
In Canada, Sail Canada Prescription 88.2 prohibits the SIs from changing the Sail Canada Prescriptions to Part 5 (protest procedures) and Rule 65.1 (liability) unless a no-appeal protest committee or international jury has been appointed.
📖
SI content requirements come from RRS Rule 90.2(a) ("The race committee shall publish written sailing instructions that conform to rule J2"), RRS Appendix J2 (the detailed content lists), and the Sail Canada Prescriptions. Items below quote the actual rule text. A rule included in the NOR need not be repeated in the SIs.

J2.1 — The SIs shall include the following (unless in the NOR) App J2.1

These seven items are mandatory in every set of SIs unless already stated in the NOR. "Shall" means no discretion.

J2.1(1) — Rules Changes and Key NOR Information ▾

Rule text: "The information in rules J1.3(1), (2) and (5) and, when applicable, rules J1.3(6), (7), (8), (9) and (10)."

Unless these were stated in the NOR, the SIs must include:

  • ★J1.3(1): Any changes to the racing rules, referring specifically to each rule and stating the change RRS 86.1
  • ★J1.3(2): Any changes to the Sail Canada Prescriptions — only permitted if a no-appeal PC or IJ is in place Sail Canada 88.2
  • ★J1.3(5): The scoring system if different from Appendix A Low Point; number of races scheduled; minimum number to constitute a series (see rule A1) RRS App A, A1
  • ◦J1.3(6) if applicable: The penalty for breaking a rule of Part 2, other than the Two-Turns Penalty RRS 44
  • ◦J1.3(7) if applicable: The time after which no warning signal will be made on the last scheduled day of racing
  • ◦J1.3(8) if applicable: Denial of the right of appeal, subject to rule 70.3 RRS 70.3
  • ◦J1.3(9) if applicable: For chartered or loaned boats, whether rule G3 applies RRS G3
  • ◦J1.3(10) if applicable: Prizes

What rule 86.1 permits and prohibits changing: The SIs may change a racing rule but not rule 4, 76.1 or 76.2(a), Appendix R, nor Part 2 or Part 7 rules, nor rules 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 42, 43, 47, 50, 63.3, 69, 70, 71, 72, 75, 76.2(b) or 79. Every change must refer specifically to the rule and state the change. RRS 86.1(b)

J2.1(2) — Schedule of Races and Warning Signal Times ▾

Rule text: "The schedule of races and the times of warning signals for each class."

Rule 26 — Starting Races: Races shall be started using the following sequence. Times shall be taken from the visual signals; the absence of a sound signal shall be disregarded:

  • ★5 minutes* — Class flag displayed, one sound = Warning signal (* or as stated in the NOR or SIs)
  • ★4 minutes — Preparatory flag (P, I, Z, Z with I, U, or Black flag) displayed, one sound = Preparatory signal
  • ★1 minute — Preparatory flag removed, one long sound = One minute signal
  • ★0 minutes — Class flag removed, one sound = Starting signal

The warning signal for each succeeding class shall be made with or after the starting signal of the preceding class. RRS 26

Race Management Manual guidance (Chapter 8): The SIs should specify a schedule that the race committee will adhere to. Create a table showing race number, date, class, and warning signal time for each class. SC RMM Ch.8

J2.1(3) — Course Description and How Courses Are Signalled ▾

Rule text: "A complete description of the course(s) to be sailed, or a list of marks from which the course will be selected and, if relevant, how courses will be signalled and any change to the direction in which boats are required to cross the finishing line to finish."

  • ★Either a complete description of all courses to be sailed, OR a list of marks from which the course will be selected
  • ★How courses will be signalled (e.g. numeral pennants, course board) if not predetermined RRS 27.1
  • ◦Any change to the direction in which boats are required to cross the finishing line to finish — if applicable

Rule 27.1: No later than the warning signal, the race committee shall signal or otherwise designate the course to be sailed if the sailing instructions have not stated the course.

Race Management Manual guidance (Chapter 7): The Race Management Manual provides detailed guidance on course types including windward/leeward, Olympic triangle, and other configurations. Approximate course length and approximate length of windward legs should also be stated — this is required under J2.2(13) if not in the NOR. SC RMM Ch.7

J2.1(4) — Descriptions of Marks ▾

Rule text: "Descriptions of marks, including starting and finishing marks, stating the order in which marks are to be passed and the side on which each is to be left and identifying all rounding marks (see the definition Sail the Course)."

  • ★Description of every mark — colour, shape, type (inflatable, buoy, vessel, etc.)
  • ★Starting mark(s) described
  • ★Finishing mark(s) described
  • ★The order in which marks are to be passed
  • ★The side on which each mark is to be left (port or starboard)
  • ★All rounding marks identified

Definition — Sail the Course: A boat sails the course by starting, sailing to each mark in the correct order, and finishing — leaving each mark on the required side. The SIs must make this unambiguous.

J2.1(5) — Starting and Finishing Lines, Class Flags, Special Signals ▾

Rule text: "Descriptions of the starting and finishing lines, class flags and any special signals to be used."

  • ★Description of the starting line — between what objects or vessels
  • ★Description of the finishing line — between what objects or vessels
  • ★Class flags to be used for each class or fleet
  • ★Any special signals used at this event not listed in the Race Signals

Rule 25.2: The meanings of the visual and sound signals stated in Race Signals shall not be changed except under rule 86.1(b). The meanings of any other signals that may be used shall be stated in the notice of race or sailing instructions.

Standard Race Signals (from Race Signals — inside front cover of RRS):

  • ▸AP — Races not started are postponed. Warning signal 1 minute after removal
  • ▸AP over A — Races not started are postponed. No more racing today
  • ▸AP over H — Races not started are postponed. Further signals ashore
  • ▸AP over numeral 1–9 — Postponement of 1–9 hours from scheduled starting time
  • ▸N — All races in progress abandoned. Return to starting area. Warning signal 1 minute after removal
  • ▸N over H — All races in progress abandoned. Further signals ashore
  • ▸N over A — All races abandoned. No more racing today
  • ▸P — Preparatory signal
  • ▸I — Rule 30.1 is in effect (boats in the triangle must be on the pre-start side of the line at the starting signal)
  • ▸Z — Rule 30.2 is in effect (20% scoring penalty for boats OCS)
  • ▸U — Rule 30.3 is in effect
  • ▸Black flag — Rule 30.4 is in effect (DSQ without hearing for boats OCS)
  • ▸First Substitute — General recall. Warning signal 1 minute after removal
  • ▸X — Individual recall (no sound)
  • ▸S — Course shortened. Rule 32.2 is in effect (no sound)
  • ▸C — Position of the next mark has been changed (with compass bearing / triangle / rectangle)
  • ▸Y — Wear a personal flotation device (see rule 40)
  • ▸V — Monitor communication channel for safety instructions (see rule 37)
  • ▸L ashore — A notice to competitors has been posted. L afloat — Come within hail or follow this vessel
  • ▸M — The object displaying this signal replaces a missing mark
  • ▸Orange flag — One end of the starting line
  • ▸Blue flag — One end of the finishing line

The SIs must state the meaning of any signal used at the event that is not in the standard Race Signals table. The standard signals above may not be changed except as permitted under rule 86.1(b).

J2.1(6) — Race Time Limit ▾

Rule text: "The race time limit, if any, for the first boat to sail the course (see rule 35)."

Rule 35 — Race Time Limit and Scores: If one boat sails the course within the time limit for that race, if any, all boats that finish shall be scored according to their finishing places unless the race is abandoned. If no boat sails the course within the race time limit, the race committee shall abandon the race.

Two distinct time limits should be considered:

  • ★The race time limit for the first boat (required under J2.1(6)) — if no limit applies, state that
  • ★The time limit for boats other than the first boat to finish — required under J2.2(18) if applicable
J2.1(7) — Official Notice Board Location ▾

Rule text: "Location(s) of official notice board(s) or address of online notice board; location of the race office."

  • ★Physical location of the official notice board (e.g. "Race Office, Main Clubhouse") OR the URL of the online notice board
  • ★Location of the race office

All official communications and SI changes are posted on the official notice board. Competitors must know where it is before they can rely on it.

J2.2 — The SIs shall include any of the following that will apply (unless in the NOR) App J2.2

These 29 items are mandatory if they apply to your event. Review every one. Omitting an applicable item is a compliance error.

J2.2(1) — Appendix P (Rule 42 Special Procedures) ▾

Rule text: "Whether Appendix P will apply." App P

Appendix P — Special Procedures for Rule 42: All or part of this appendix applies only if the notice of race or sailing instructions so state. If Appendix P applies:

  • ▸First penalty (P2.1): Two-Turns Penalty under rule 44.2. Failure to take it = DSQ without hearing
  • ▸Second penalty (P2.2): Boat shall promptly retire. Failure = DSQ without hearing, score not excludable
  • ▸Third and subsequent penalties (P2.3): Boat shall retire. DSQ without hearing, score not excludable. Failure = DSQ from all races, no score excluded, protest committee shall consider rule 69.2
  • ▸Postponement/general recall/abandonment (P3): Cancels the penalty but it is still counted toward the total for the event
J2.2(2) — National Prescriptions in English for Foreign Entries ▾

Rule text: "When appropriate, at an event where entries from other countries are expected, a copy in English of the national prescriptions that will apply." RRS 90.2(b)

Rule 90.2(b) reinforces this: "When appropriate, for an event where entries from other countries are expected, the sailing instructions shall include, in English, the applicable national prescriptions."

J2.2(3) — Procedure for Changing the Sailing Instructions ▾

Rule text: "Procedure for changing the sailing instructions." RRS 90.2(c)

Rule 90.2(c): The sailing instructions may be changed provided the change is in writing and posted on the official notice board before the time stated in the sailing instructions, or on the water, communicated to each boat before her warning signal. Oral changes may be given only on the water, and only if the procedure is stated in the sailing instructions.

Race Management Manual guidance (Chapter 4): Recommended wording: "Any change to the sailing instructions will be posted before 0900 on the day it will take effect, except that any change to the schedule of races will be posted by 2000 on the day before it will take effect." Adjust times to allow competitors adequate notice. SC RMM Ch.4

J2.2(4) — Procedure for Oral Changes on the Water ▾

Rule text: "Procedure for giving oral changes to the sailing instructions on the water (see rule 90.2(c))."

Oral changes may only be given on the water, and only if the procedure is stated in the sailing instructions. If oral changes are permitted, state the method (e.g., by VHF to all boats on channel [X] before the warning signal).

J2.2(5) — Safety Requirements: PFDs, Check-In, Check-Out ▾

Rule text: "Safety requirements, such as requirements and signals for personal flotation devices, check-in at the starting area, and check-out and check-in ashore."

Rule 40 — Personal Flotation Devices:

  • ★Rule 40.1: When rule 40.1 is made applicable, each competitor shall wear a personal flotation device except briefly while changing or adjusting clothing or personal equipment. Wet suits and dry suits are NOT personal flotation devices.
  • ★Rule 40.2: Rule 40.1 applies if (a) flag Y was displayed afloat with one sound before or with the warning signal; (b) flag Y was displayed ashore with one sound, at all times while afloat that day; or (c) a rule in the class rules, notice of race or sailing instructions states that it applies.

If PFDs are required at all times while afloat (not just when flag Y is displayed), state this explicitly in the SIs. State the minimum standard of PFD required (e.g., Transport Canada approved).

Race Management Manual warning (Chapter 4): If a race officer makes one comment about one PFD, they could be deemed to have inspected and decided upon the adequacy of all. Rule 44 — Penalties at the Time of an Incident ▾

Rule 44.1 — Taking a Penalty: A boat may take a Two-Turns Penalty when she may have broken one or more rules of Part 2 in an incident while racing. She may take a One-Turn Penalty when she may have broken rule 31. Alternatively, the notice of race or sailing instructions may specify the use of the Scoring Penalty or some other penalty, in which case the specified penalty shall replace the One-Turn and Two-Turns Penalty.

Rule 44.2 — One-Turn and Two-Turns Penalties: After getting well clear of other boats as soon after the incident as possible, a boat takes a One-Turn or Two-Turns Penalty by promptly making the required number of turns in the same direction, each turn including one tack and one gybe. When a boat takes the penalty at or near the finishing line, her hull shall be completely on the course side of the line before she finishes.

Rule 44.3 — Scoring Penalty: If specified in the NOR or SIs, a boat takes a Scoring Penalty by displaying a yellow flag at the first reasonable opportunity after the incident, keeping the flag displayed until finishing, and calling the race committee's attention to it at the finishing line. She shall also inform the race committee of the identity of the other boat involved.

  • ◦Exception: regardless of the penalty in the SIs, if the boat caused injury or serious damage or gained a significant advantage despite taking a penalty, her penalty shall be to retire RRS 44.1(b)

Fundamental Rules — Relevant to Race Organization

Rule 4 — Decision to Race ▾

Full rule text: "The responsibility for a boat's decision to participate in a race or to continue racing is hers alone."

This rule is frequently cited in the SIs as a liability disclaimer. It cannot be changed by the NOR or SIs (rule 86.1(b) prohibits changing rule 4).

Rule 25 — Notice of Race, Sailing Instructions and Signals ▾

25.1: The notice of race shall be made available to each boat that enters an event before she enters. The sailing instructions shall be made available to each boat before a race begins.

25.2: The meanings of the visual and sound signals stated in Race Signals shall not be changed except under rule 86.1(b). The meanings of any other signals that may be used shall be stated in the notice of race or sailing instructions.

25.3: When the race committee is required to display a flag as a visual signal, it may use a flag or other object of a similar appearance.

Rule 26 — Starting Races ▾

Full rule text: "Races shall be started by using the following signals. Times shall be taken from the visual signals; the absence of a sound signal shall be disregarded."

Rules 27 & 28 — Race Committee Actions Before Start / Sailing the Course ▾

27.1: No later than the warning signal, the race committee shall signal or otherwise designate the course to be sailed if the sailing instructions have not stated the course, and it may replace one course signal with another and signal that wearing personal flotation devices is required (display flag Y with one sound).

27.2: No later than the preparatory signal, the race committee may move a starting mark.

27.3: Before the starting signal, the race committee may for any reason postpone (display flag AP, AP over H, or AP over A, with two sounds) or abandon the race (display flag N, N over H, or N over A, with three sounds).

28.1: A boat shall sail the course.

28.2: A boat may correct any errors in sailing the course, provided she has not finished.

Rules 29 & 30 — Recalls and Starting Penalties ▾

29.1 — Individual Recall: When at a boat's starting signal any part of her hull is on the course side of the starting line, the race committee shall promptly display flag X with one sound. Flag X shall be displayed until each such boat has been completely on the pre-start side of the starting line, but no later than four minutes after the starting signal or one minute before any later starting signal, whichever is earlier.

29.2 — General Recall: When at the starting signal the race committee is unable to identify boats on the course side, or there has been an error in the starting procedure, the race committee may signal a general recall (First Substitute, two sounds). The warning signal for a new start shall be made one minute after the First Substitute is removed (one sound).

30.1 — I Flag Rule: If flag I has been displayed, any part of a boat's hull on the course side of the starting line or its extensions during the last minute before her starting signal shall sail across an extension so that her hull is completely on the pre-start side before she starts.

30.2 — Z Flag Rule: No part of a boat's hull shall be in the triangle formed by the ends of the starting line and the first mark during the last minute before her starting signal. Penalty: 20% Scoring Penalty without a hearing, even if the race is restarted or resailed, but not if postponed or abandoned before the starting signal.

30.3 — U Flag Rule: Same triangle restriction as 30.2. Penalty: disqualification without a hearing, but not if the race is restarted or resailed.

30.4 — Black Flag Rule: Same triangle restriction. Penalty: disqualification without a hearing, even if the race is restarted or resailed, but not if postponed or abandoned before the starting signal. The race committee shall display the boat's sail number before the next warning signal.

Rules 32 & 33 — Shortening/Abandonment After the Start; Changing the Course ▾

32.1: After the starting signal, the race committee may shorten the course or abandon the race: (a) because of foul weather; (b) because of insufficient wind making it unlikely any boat will finish within the time limit; (c) because a mark is missing or out of position; or (d) for any other reason directly affecting the safety or fairness of the competition. Also: to shorten course so other races can be sailed, or to abandon because of an error in the starting procedure. However, after one boat has finished within the time limit, if any, the race committee shall not abandon the race without considering the consequences for all boats.

32.2: To shorten the course, the race committee shall display flag S with two sounds before the first boat crosses the finishing line. The finishing line shall be at a rounding mark (between mark and a staff displaying flag S), at a line the course requires boats to cross, or at a gate.

32.3: To abandon a race in progress, the race committee shall display flag N, N over H, or N over A, with three sounds.

33: While boats are racing, the race committee may change a leg of the course that begins at a rounding mark or gate by changing the position of the next mark and signalling all boats before they begin the leg. Signal: flag C with repetitive sounds and (a) new compass bearing and/or green triangle (starboard change) or red rectangle (port change); or (b) '–' if length decreased, '+' if increased.

Rule 34 — Mark Missing ▾

Full rule text: If a mark is missing or out of position while boats are racing, the race committee shall, if possible, (a) move it to its correct position or replace it with a new one of similar appearance, or (b) replace it with an object displaying flag M and make repetitive sounds.

Rule 35 — Race Time Limit and Scores ▾

Full rule text: "If one boat sails the course within the time limit for that race, if any, all boats that finish shall be scored according to their finishing places unless the race is abandoned. If no boat sails the course within the race time limit, the race committee shall abandon the race."

The SIs should state two time limits: (1) the time limit for the first boat to complete the course (J2.1(6)); and (2) the time limit for all other boats to finish after the first finisher (J2.2(18)). Boats that do not finish within the applicable time limit are scored DNF.

Rule 37 — Search and Rescue Instructions ▾

Full rule text: "When the race committee displays flag V with one sound, all boats and official and support vessels shall, if possible, monitor the race committee communication channel for search and rescue instructions."

Rule 40 — Personal Flotation Devices ▾

40.1 — Basic Rule: When rule 40.1 is made applicable by rule 40.2, each competitor shall wear a personal flotation device except briefly while changing or adjusting clothing or personal equipment. Wet suits and dry suits are not personal flotation devices.

40.2 — When Rule 40.1 Applies: Rule 40.1 applies if (a) flag Y was displayed afloat with one sound before or with the warning signal, while racing in that race; (b) flag Y was displayed ashore with one sound, at all times while afloat that day; or (c) a rule in the class rules, notice of race or sailing instructions states that it applies.

Rule 41 — Outside Help ▾

Full rule text: "A boat shall not receive help from any outside source, except (a) help for a crew member who is ill, injured or in danger; (b) after a collision, help from the crew of the other vessel to get clear; (c) help in the form of information freely available to all boats; (d) unsolicited information from a disinterested source, which may be another boat in the same race."

Rule 42 — Propulsion (Summary) ▾

42.1 — Basic Rule: Except when permitted in rule 42.3 or 45, a boat shall compete by using only the wind and water to increase, maintain or decrease her speed. Her crew may adjust the trim of sails and hull, and perform other acts of seamanship, but shall not otherwise move their bodies to propel the boat.

42.2 — Prohibited Actions: Pumping, rocking, ooching, sculling, and repeated tacks or gybes unrelated to changes in wind or tactical considerations.

42.3(i): Sailing instructions may, in stated circumstances, permit propulsion using an engine or any other method, provided the boat does not gain a significant advantage in the race. This must be stated in the SIs under J2.2(8).

Rule 44 — Penalties at the Time of an Incident ▾

44.1: A boat may take a Two-Turns Penalty when she may have broken one or more rules of Part 2 in an incident while racing. She may take a One-Turn Penalty when she may have broken rule 31. Alternatively, the notice of race or sailing instructions may specify the use of the Scoring Penalty or some other penalty, in which case the specified penalty shall replace the One-Turn and Two-Turns Penalty. However, if the boat caused injury or serious damage or gained a significant advantage in the race or series by her breach, her penalty shall be to retire.

44.2: After getting well clear of other boats as soon after the incident as possible, a boat takes a One-Turn or Two-Turns Penalty by promptly making the required number of turns in the same direction, each turn including one tack and one gybe. When a boat takes the penalty at or near the finishing line, her hull shall be completely on the course side of the line before she finishes.

44.3 — Scoring Penalty: If specified in the NOR or SIs, a boat takes a Scoring Penalty by displaying a yellow flag at the first reasonable opportunity after the incident and keeping it displayed until finishing.

Rule 60.2 & 60.3 — Protest: Intention and Delivery ▾

60.2(a) — Intention to Protest: If a protest concerns an incident observed by the protestor in the racing area: (1) If the protestor is a boat, she shall hail "Protest" and, if her hull length is longer than 6 metres, conspicuously display a red flag, at the first reasonable opportunity for each. She shall display the flag until she is no longer racing. (2) If the protestor is a committee, it shall inform the boat after the race within the protest time limit of its intention to protest her.

60.3(a): When delivered, a protest shall be in writing and identify the protestor, the protestee, and the incident.

60.3(b) — Default Protest Time Limit: A protest shall be delivered to the race office within the protest time limit unless the protest committee decides there is good reason to extend the time. The default protest time limit is (1) for protests about an incident observed in the racing area: two hours after the last boat in the race finishes; or (2) for other protests: two hours after the relevant information is available. If the sailing instructions state a different protest time limit, that time limit applies instead.

Rule 61 — Redress ▾

61.1: A boat may request redress. The race committee or technical committee may request redress for a boat. The protest committee may call a hearing to consider redress.

61.2 — Requests for Redress: A request for redress shall be in writing and identify the reason. It shall be delivered to the race office: (1) if based on an incident in the racing area, within the protest time limit or two hours after the incident (whichever is later); (2) if based on a protest committee decision on the last scheduled day of racing, no later than 30 minutes after the decision was posted; (3) for all other requests, as soon as reasonably possible after the relevant information is available. The protest committee shall extend the time if there is good reason.

61.4(b) — Grounds for Redress: A boat is entitled to redress if her score has been made significantly worse through no fault of her own by: (1) an improper action or omission of a committee or the organizing authority; (2) injury or physical damage from a boat that was breaking a rule of Part 2 and took a penalty or was penalized; (3) injury or physical damage from a vessel not racing that was required to keep clear; (4) giving help in compliance with rule 1.1; or (5) an action of another boat or support person resulting in a penalty under rule 2 or 69.

Rule 70 — Appeals and Requests to a National Authority ▾

70.1: Unless rule 70.3 applies, a party to a hearing has the right to appeal the protest committee's decision or its procedures, but not the facts found, to the national authority. In addition, a party may appeal when the protest committee has failed to hold a hearing or to make a decision.

70.3 — When There Is No Right to Appeal: There is no right to appeal a decision of an international jury constituted in accordance with rule N1. In addition, a national authority may prescribe that the right of appeal may be denied in specific circumstances (a) when so stated in the sailing instructions, provided that (i) it is essential to determine promptly the result of a race that will qualify a boat to compete in a subsequent race or event; (ii) a national authority so prescribes for a particular event; or (b) when so stated in the sailing instructions for a particular event, provided that the national authority approves and an international jury is not required.

Sail Canada Prescription 70.3(b) so prescribes. Sail Canada Prescription to Appendix R: Appeals shall be submitted to Sail Canada at [email protected]. Sail Canada may delegate to a provincial appeals committee. Decisions of a provincial appeals committee may be further appealed to Sail Canada.

Rules 75 & 76 — Entry and Exclusion ▾

75 — Entering an Event: To enter an event, a boat shall comply with the requirements of the organizing authority of the event. She shall be entered by (a) a member of a club or other organization affiliated to a World Sailing member national authority; (b) such a club or organization; or (c) a member of a World Sailing member national authority.

76.1 — Exclusion: The organizing authority or the race committee may reject or cancel the entry of a boat or exclude a competitor, provided (a) it does so before the start of the first race after receipt of the entry; and (b) it states a proper reason. The reason shall be provided promptly in writing if requested.

76.2: However, the organizing authority or race committee shall not reject or cancel the entry or exclude a competitor (a) because of advertising if the boat complies with the World Sailing Advertising Code; or (b) at world and continental championships if the entry is within stated quotas and has approval of the relevant World Sailing Class Association.

Rules 85 & 86 — Changes to Rules ▾

85.1: A change to a rule shall refer specifically to the rule and state the change. A change to a rule includes an addition to it or deletion of all or part of it.

85.2 — How Rules May Be Changed:

  • ▸Racing rule → only if permitted by rule 86
  • ▸National authority prescription → only by rule 88.2
  • ▸Class rule → only by rule 87
  • ▸Rule in the notice of race → only by rule 89.2(b)
  • ▸Rule in the sailing instructions → only by rule 90.2(c)

86.1 — Changes to Racing Rules: A racing rule shall not be changed unless permitted in the rule itself or as follows: (a) Prescriptions of a national authority may change a racing rule, but not the Definitions; a rule in the Introduction; Part 2 or 7; rule 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 42, 43, 47, 50, 63.3, 69, 70, 71, 72, 75, 76.2(b) or 79; a rule of an appendix that changes one of these rules; Appendix H or N; or a rule in a World Sailing Code listed in rule 6.1. (b) The notice of race or sailing instructions may change a racing rule, but not rule 4, 76.1 or 76.2(a), Appendix R, or a rule listed in rule 86.1(a).

Rules 87 & 88 — Changes to Class Rules; National Prescriptions ▾

87 — Changes to Class Rules: The notice of race may change a class rule only when the class rules permit the change, or when written permission of the class association for the change is posted on the official notice board.

88.1 — Prescriptions That Apply: The prescriptions that apply to an event are the prescriptions of the national authority with which the organizing authority is associated under rule 89.1. However, if boats will pass through the waters of more than one national authority while racing, the notice of race shall identify the prescriptions that will apply and when they will apply.

88.2 — Changes to Prescriptions: The notice of race or sailing instructions may change a prescription. However, a national authority may restrict changes to its prescriptions with a prescription to this rule, provided World Sailing approves its application to do so. The restricted prescriptions shall not be changed. Sail Canada has so prescribed — its prescriptions to Part 5 and Rule 65.1 cannot be changed or deleted.

Rules 89 & 90 — Organizing Authority; Sailing Instructions; Scoring ▾

89.2(a): The organizing authority shall publish a written notice of race that conforms to rule J1.

89.2(b): The notice of race may be changed provided adequate notice is given.

89.2(c): The organizing authority shall appoint a race committee and, when appropriate, appoint a protest committee, a technical committee and umpires.

90.2(a): The race committee shall publish written sailing instructions that conform to rule J2.

90.2(b): When appropriate, for an event where entries from other countries are expected, the sailing instructions shall include, in English, the applicable national prescriptions.

90.2(c): The sailing instructions may be changed provided the change is in writing and posted on the official notice board before the time stated in the sailing instructions or, on the water, communicated to each boat before her warning signal. Oral changes may be given only on the water, and only if the procedure is stated in the sailing instructions.

90.3(a) — Scoring: The race committee shall score a race or series as provided in Appendix A unless the NOR or SIs specify some other system. A race shall be scored if it is not abandoned and if one boat sails the course within the race time limit, even if she retires after finishing or is disqualified.

90.3(e): When so stated in the NOR, notwithstanding other provisions, there shall be no changes to race or series scores resulting from action initiated more than 24 hours after the later of: (1) the protest time limit for the last race; (2) being informed of a protest committee decision after the last race; or (3) the event results being published. Exception: changes made under rule 6, 69 or 71.

What the Manual Says About the Notice of Race Chapter 3

The RO's Role in Drafting the NOR

The manual states that "Race officers should help to draft the notice of race, expressing their views on the relevant items listed in RRS [J1]." This means the RO should be appointed before the NOR is finalized, not after. The RO's early involvement is a best practice requirement, not optional.

The best practice is that the organizing authority and the senior race officer work closely on both the NOR and the SIs together. This puts a premium on early appointment of the race committee.

RRS Appendix KG — Use the Template

RRS Appendix KG is the recommended NOR template wording. The manual states the RO should use this guide when writing the NOR. It provides specific wording for each required item in RRS Appendix J1.

Practical Information Section — Separate from the NOR

A separate section entitled "Practical Information Not Part of the NoR" should be included for items that may be distributed with the NOR but are not part of it. The manual lists examples:

  • ◦List of sponsors, if appropriate
  • ◦Lodging and camping information
  • ◦Parking information
  • ◦Description of meal facilities
  • ◦Special mooring or storage requirements
  • ◦Sail and boat repair facilities and ship's chandlers
  • ◦Availability of chartered or loaned boats
NOR Revisions — Handle with Care

The NOR may be revised up until the event date but any revisions to an already published NOR must be conveyed to those who have already entered, as you may have changed their conditions of entry. NOR revisions should always have a version date/number clearly indicated. Revisions can be done as formal amendments posted on the official notice board — which must itself be listed in the NOR.

Three Lists in RRS Appendix J1

The manual explains that RRS Appendix J1 includes three lists:

  • ★J1.1 — Information the NOR shall include: where and when the event occurs, conditions of competition
  • ◦J1.2 — Information that applies depending on the complexity of the event (12 items that must now be in the NOR, not the SIs, per the 2025-2028 changes)
  • ◦J1.3 — Information that would help competitors decide whether to attend, and other helpful information

The manual notes that if a rule is mentioned in the NOR, it does not need to be repeated in the SIs.

What the Manual Says About Sailing Instructions Chapter 4

The RO as Principal Author

The manual states: "The race officer (RO) should be the principal author of the sailing instructions, using Appendix LG, Sailing Instructions Guide on the Sail Canada and World Sailing websites, and any class rules governing the conduct of the event." The RO should seek the views of the class liaison person, the chairman of the jury or protest committee, and the general chairman before the final draft is agreed.

Drafting can commence immediately after the Race Officer is appointed. The process should continue throughout the preparation period and the final version must be published on the event website or distributed at registration.

RRS Appendix LG — Use the Template. Do Not Ad Lib.

RRS Appendix LG provides the SI template. The manual is direct: "Race officers should use the specific language in that Appendix whenever possible. DO NOT AD LIB." Paragraphs in Appendix LG marked with an asterisk (*) are required by rule J2.1 unless they are already in the NOR.

Seven Mandatory SI Items Under RRS J2.1

The manual identifies seven mandatory items required in the SIs by RRS J2.1 (unless already in the NOR):

  • ★The information in rules J1.3(1), (2) and (5) and, when applicable, rules J1.3(6), (7), (8), (9) and (10)
  • ★The schedule of races and the times of warning signals for each class
  • ★A complete description of the course(s) to be sailed, or a list of marks from which the course will be selected, and how courses will be signalled
  • ★Descriptions of marks, including starting and finishing marks
  • ★The sailing instructions must address all items in RRS J2.1 — see the full list in RRS Appendix J RRS J2.1
Changes to SIs — Timing Rules

The manual provides recommended wording for the SI change provision: "Any change to the sailing instructions will be posted before 0900 on the day it will take effect, except that any change to the schedule of races will be posted by 2000 on the day before it will take effect."

The times must allow enough time for a competitor to learn of changes. The times listed in the guide are examples only — adjust for your event.

Jury Review of the SIs

The manual states that prior to the event, the RO should ask the jury to review the notice of race and sailing instructions to determine if there are any issues that require resolution. A jury on the water can also monitor race procedures and can assist and advise — but not supervise or direct the RO.

On-Water Operations Guidance

The Competitors Meeting Ch. 14 ▾

The meeting is called the Competitors Meeting, not the Skippers Meeting — all sailors, parents, press, and officials are invited. It must be well-prepared and brief. The RO must not interpret the sailing instructions at this meeting. If a question is raised about the SIs, questions must be put to the RO in writing, answered in writing, and both posted on the official notice board.

Agenda items include: welcome; sponsor acknowledgements; schedule; introduction of officials; location of notice board, protest desk, first aid; identification of race committee vessels; racing area hazards; wind and weather briefing; question-and-answer period.

Record Keeping Ch. 16 ▾

The manual states records must include: sail numbers of boats that arrive at the starting area; boats that start prematurely or in violation of RRS 30; any boats involved in apparent rule infringements; the order of finish; any protest flags observed or reports of protest intentions received at the finish. Voice recorders should be used when possible.

Abandonment Ch. 10 ▾

A race may be abandoned before it starts (RRS 27.3), while in progress (RRS 32), or after boats have finished (RRS 32). The manual recommends: do not abandon a race after one or more boats have properly finished. Three abandonment signal variants exist: flag N (all races abandoned, return to start); flag N over H (abandoned, further signals will be made); flag N over A (abandoned, no further racing today).

Course Configuration Ch. 11 ▾

Maintaining course configuration after the start is one of the committee's most important tasks. The manual states that a wind shift of only 15–20 degrees can reduce a dead run to a one-tack reaching leg, eliminating strategic and tactical challenges. A 30-degree shift on a windward leg cuts port-tack sailing by 62% and reduces usable course area by 50%. Corrective action requires relocating marks and signalling changes per RRS Appendix N.

Starting Problems Ch. 9 ▾

The fundamental cause of general recalls is a poor starting line — either too short or not perpendicular to the wind. Beyond about 15–20 degrees from square, one end becomes heavily favoured and the fleet crowds it. The manual recommends: adjust the line right up to the preparatory signal; use RRS 30.1 (I Flag) for fleets over 30 yachts; make individual recall signals as soon as possible; maintain good radio contact between all boats.

Coach Vessel Control Ch. 12 ▾

From the moment a boat is racing — from her preparatory signal until after she finishes — there can be no contact or communication between her and her coach vessel. For any significant event, the NOR should require that coach vessels register with the Event Office and receive an identifying pennant to display at all times while afloat.

Committee Vessel Protocol Ch. 17 ▾

The committee vessel is for working committee members only. The RO should insist that no one other than working committee members are aboard. Jury members should be on separate, clearly marked jury vessels so they cannot attempt to influence the RO's decision-making and so the race committee is seen to be independent of the jury.

Liaisons: Jury, Class Rep, Scorer Ch. 15 ▾

The RO must establish and maintain relationships with: the event chair (overall accountability); the class representative (class requirements, measurement questions, course conditions advice during the event); the jury chair (review of NOR and SIs before the event, daily communication, understanding respective roles); and the chief scorer (method of scoring, how results will be communicated). Brief daily meetings with most of these officials are productive.
